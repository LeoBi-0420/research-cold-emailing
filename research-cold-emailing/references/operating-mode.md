# Mail operating mode

Read this reference only when the task involves a connected mail account, attachments, follow-ups, sending, or mail-account batch work.

## Permission ladder

Treat these as separate scopes:

1. research public sources;
2. draft text in the conversation or a local file;
3. create or edit a draft in a connected mail account;
4. send mail.

An instruction authorizing one level does not authorize a higher level. A saved preference, profile, prior batch approval, or this skill never authorizes sending.

If no compatible mail tool or connected account is available, do not simulate a saved draft or sent message. Return the approved text locally, identify the unavailable action, and preserve the user's authorization boundary for a later attempt.

## Draft creation

Before creating a mail draft, confirm:

- the intended mail account or connected service;
- the individual recipient or approved source list;
- the content rules;
- the approved attachment and exact file, if any;
- whether the task is first contact or a follow-up.

For each draft:

1. Check Drafts and Sent Items, plus the existing thread when available.
2. Use only an address explicitly published by an authoritative source and confirm the mail service resolves the intended person.
3. Enter the approved subject and complete body.
4. Attach only the explicitly approved file.
5. Wait for the service to save.
6. Leave the compose view, reopen the saved draft, and inspect the recipient, subject, complete body, and every required attachment.
7. Close the compose view before starting another draft.

A compose window, draft count, success notification, or visible attachment during upload is not proof that the complete draft persisted.

## Sending authorization

Sending requires a current instruction that states:

- the individual recipients or approved source list;
- the maximum number of messages;
- the content rules or approved drafts;
- the attachment rules;
- the timing.

Do not expand beyond that scope. Do not ask for repeated approval when every required detail is already explicit and unchanged.

Immediately before each send, inspect the recipient, subject, complete body, attachment state, and account. Stop when any field differs from the approved content or cannot be inspected.

## Deduplication and follow-ups

- Do not create another first-contact email when Drafts or Sent Items already contains one for the same professor.
- Use the existing thread for a follow-up.
- Verify the prior message and date before drafting the follow-up.
- Do not treat a reply, meeting invitation, or out-of-office message as permission to send unrelated material.

## Client consistency

Use one mail client for the duration of an automated run. Do not edit the same draft concurrently in another browser tab, desktop application, or device.

## Stop conditions

Stop the affected message or batch for:

- login expiry, multifactor authentication, CAPTCHA, or a new permission prompt;
- unresolved or ambiguous recipient identity;
- missing or wrong attachment;
- incomplete or changed body text;
- unclear sending scope;
- conflicting duplicate records;
- inability to reopen and verify the saved draft.

Record the exact reason. Do not downgrade to a less specific or less verified message just to finish.
