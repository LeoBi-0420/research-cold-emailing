# Professor research method

Read this reference for professor discovery, source verification, research briefs, and paper-level personalization.

## Resolve identity first

Confirm the following from a current official university page whenever possible:

- full name;
- institution and department or school;
- current title;
- official profile URL;
- explicitly published email address.

Do not merge people with similar names. Do not construct an address from an institutional pattern.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order, adjusting when the field publishes differently:

1. official university faculty, lab, center, or working-paper page;
2. author-hosted paper or current curriculum vitae;
3. publisher page, DOI record, journal page, conference page, or institutional repository;
4. established preprint repository;
5. scholarly index or search result used only to locate a stronger source.

Use news stories, interviews, and secondary summaries as context, not as the sole support for a technical claim when a primary source is available.

If current web access is unavailable, do not rely on model memory for identity, contact information, currentness, or opportunity evidence. Ask the user for official URLs or source files, or return a clearly labeled research blocker.

## Currentness labels

Label the evidence accurately:

- **current project:** an official current page or recent dated source explicitly presents it as ongoing;
- **working paper:** the author or institution lists it as a working paper;
- **recent publication:** a dated publication, without implying that the project is ongoing;
- **established research area:** supported by a faculty profile or publication history;
- **uncertain current status:** relevance is plausible but current activity is not established.

Do not turn `recent`, `listed`, or `published` into `currently working on`.

## Read for an email-sized idea

Look for one detail that a student can understand and react to honestly:

- a counterintuitive finding;
- a research design or dataset;
- a tension between two explanations;
- a limitation or unanswered question;
- a real-world decision affected by the result;
- a method the student wants to learn.

The goal is not to summarize the professor's career. It is to find one defensible reason for this student to contact this professor.

## Keep research fit and opportunity evidence separate

Never use research relevance as evidence that a professor is recruiting.

### Research fit

`Research fit` measures the relationship between the professor's verified work and the student's approved interests, preparation, or learning goals.

Use one of these values:

- `strong`: a specific research question or method connects clearly to the student's verified interests or preparation;
- `moderate`: a genuine connection exists, but it is broad, exploratory, or method-based;
- `weak`: the connection depends mainly on generic field labels or unrelated credentials;
- `insufficient evidence`: available sources do not support a reliable assessment.

### Current opportunity evidence

`Current opportunity evidence` measures whether a current source indicates that the professor or group may be taking students. It does not measure research relevance.

Use one of these values:

- `confirmed opening`: a current official listing or a direct message from the professor explicitly confirms an available role;
- `current invitation`: a current official page explicitly invites students to inquire or says the group is looking for students, without confirming a specific role;
- `none found`: no current public or direct evidence of availability was found;
- `stale or conflicting`: a source is old, undated, expired, or conflicts with another source;
- `not checked`: opportunity evidence was outside the requested research scope.

`none found` means absence of evidence, not evidence that no opportunity exists.

### Contact decision

- Strong or moderate research fit can justify a truthful exploratory inquiry even when current opportunity evidence is `none found`.
- Without opportunity evidence, ask whether help might be useful; do not write as though an opening, position, lab vacancy, or active recruitment process exists.
- With a `current invitation`, refer only to the invitation actually stated.
- With a `confirmed opening`, name the role and source accurately.
- Weak research fit plus no opportunity evidence is normally a low-priority contact because the email would lack a specific reason for reaching out.

Sending more well-researched exploratory inquiries may improve the chance of discovering unadvertised opportunities. It does not change the evidence level for any individual professor.

## Evidence record

For each professor, record:

```text
Professor:
Institution and unit:
Official profile:
Official email source:
Research source title:
Research source URL:
Source type and date:
Exact supported detail:
What was actually reviewed:
Currentness label:
Student connection:
Research fit: strong | moderate | weak | insufficient evidence
Research-fit reason:
Current opportunity evidence: confirmed opening | current invitation | none found | stale or conflicting | not checked
Opportunity source and date:
Uncertainties or conflicts:
```

Keep the `Exact supported detail` narrow enough that another reviewer can trace it to the source. If the source is a PDF, record the page, section, figure, or table when available.

## Discovery and fit

When discovering professors, rank relevance using observable signals rather than prestige:

- connection to the student's stated questions or methods;
- presence of work the student can discuss honestly;
- plausible entry-level tasks supported by the student's verified preparation;
- source quality and currentness;
- available, official contact information.

Treat the ranking as prioritization, not proof that the professor is accepting students. Store research fit and opportunity evidence in separate fields rather than combining them into a single match score.

## When to stop

Do not produce a `content-ready` draft when the research detail or student connection cannot be supported. Do not produce a `mail-ready` result when the recipient cannot be verified. Relevant blockers include:

- identity cannot be resolved;
- no authoritative email source exists when a mail action or mail-ready result is requested;
- the research detail cannot be traced to a source;
- sources materially conflict and the conflict cannot be resolved;
- only generic research keywords are available and the draft would pretend otherwise.

Return the research completed, the uncertainty, and the next evidence needed.
