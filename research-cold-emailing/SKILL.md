# Research Cold Emailing

Turn public faculty research and a user-approved student profile into concise, honest outreach. Optimize for factual integrity, genuine intellectual connection, and an easy reply—not maximum volume.

## Route the task

Read only the references needed for the current request:

- **Professor discovery or research:** read [references/research-method.md](references/research-method.md).
- **Drafting or rewriting:** read [references/research-method.md](references/research-method.md) and the complete [email drafting guide](references/writing-rules.md). The guide defines the inputs, research-angle selection, paragraph-by-paragraph method, examples, tone, and final drafting check. Read [references/example-first-contact.md](references/example-first-contact.md) when a worked evidence-to-email example would help.
- **Reviewing an existing draft:** read [references/research-method.md](references/research-method.md), [references/writing-rules.md](references/writing-rules.md), and [references/quality-review.md](references/quality-review.md).
- **Multiple professors or unattended preparation:** also read [references/batch-workflow.md](references/batch-workflow.md).
- **Creating mail drafts, attaching files, following up, or sending:** also read [references/operating-mode.md](references/operating-mode.md).
- **Student facts:** use a profile supplied by the user. If none exists, use [references/student-profile.example.md](references/student-profile.example.md) only as a schema and ask for the material facts that affect the draft. Never fill blanks by inference.

## Core workflow

1. **Define the action boundary.** Distinguish research, text drafting, mail-draft creation, and sending. Each is a separate action; permission for one does not imply permission for another.
2. **Resolve the person.** Confirm the professor's identity, institution, role, official profile, and explicitly published email address. Never construct or infer an address.
3. **Build an evidence record.** Identify the exact source and the smallest research claim it supports. Score `Research fit` and `Current opportunity evidence` independently. Separate established work from research shown as current.
4. **Find one real connection.** Use a specific result, method, tension, dataset, or question. Connect it to one verified element of the student's preparation or curiosity.
5. **Draft at the evidence level available.** Do not imply that the student read more than was actually reviewed. Prefer a modest, concrete claim over impressive-sounding general language.
6. **Review before action.** Apply the quality gate. Stop when the evidence cannot support a truthful, specific message.
7. **Verify any mail-account result.** Reopen saved drafts and inspect the recipient, subject, full body, and required attachments before reporting success or sending.

## Drafting contract

Every first-contact email must contain:

1. a direct student identity and purpose;
2. one traceable research detail;
3. the student's genuine reaction, question, or reason for caring;
4. one verified preparation example connected to the research;
5. one small, easy-to-answer request;
6. the approved closing and signature.

Do not draft from research keywords alone. Do not use a fixed template without adapting the reasoning connecting the source, the student's reaction, and the preparation example.

## Non-negotiable boundaries

- A request to research, summarize, draft text, review, or create a local file does not authorize a mail-account action.
- Creating a draft in a mail account requires an explicit request naming the account or connected service.
- Sending requires a current, bounded instruction specifying the recipients or approved source list, maximum count, content rules, attachment rules, and timing.
- Never reuse an earlier sending approval for a different batch.
- Never claim that a professor has an opening, that work is ongoing, or that the student read a full paper without supporting evidence.
- Never fabricate a citation, email address, student fact, research interpretation, or attachment state.
- Do not weaken the evidence standard to finish a batch.
- Stop for unresolved recipients, missing required attachments, changed content, authentication challenges, CAPTCHAs, or unclear authorization.

## Expected deliverables

Match the output to the requested mode. Do not add an email draft to a professor-discovery-only request or perform mail actions during a text-drafting request.

For a complete research-and-draft request, return:

1. a compact research brief with source links and uncertainties;
2. separate `Research fit` and `Current opportunity evidence` fields;
3. a subject line and email body;
4. a short note explaining which research detail and student proof point were used;
5. the quality-gate result;
6. any blockers that keep the result from being `content-ready` or `mail-ready`.
